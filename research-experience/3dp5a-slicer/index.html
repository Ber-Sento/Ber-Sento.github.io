Development of a 3D Model Slicer for a 5-Axis 3D Printer

Undergraduate Thesis | Multi-Axis Additive Manufacturing

2026

Project Overview

This undergraduate thesis focused on developing a dedicated software slicer for a five-axis 3D printer. Conventional three-axis 3D printing deposits material in planar layers, which can require support structures when manufacturing geometries with overhanging features.

The objective was to develop a slicing workflow that could account for the printer's additional rotational axes. The resulting software implemented segment-based slicing, orientation planning, coordinate transformations, and G-code processing to generate machine-compatible instructions for the 3DP-5A printer.

My Contribution

I independently developed the complete slicer software, from researching the underlying slicing algorithms to implementing the processing workflow and graphical user interface. This included integrating PrusaSlicer as the standard slicing engine and developing additional processing stages to adapt its output for the five-axis machine.

The printer hardware was developed separately by my teammate. We collaborated during the physical testing of the system.

Methodology and Software Development

The development followed an iterative research and development process. I studied conventional slicing fundamentals, investigated possible algorithmic approaches, tested them using STL models, and refined the software based on the observed results. The implementation was then integrated with the printer for G-code and physical testing.

Segment-Based Slicing

The STL model was divided into segments according to changes in orientation. Each segment was processed through a slicing workflow that used coordinate transformations to align its geometry for planar slicing.

Center Layer Generation

Layer center points were generated to support orientation planning. By comparing the positions of successive layer centers, the software determined the orientation needed for the corresponding segment.

Orientation Planning and Coordinate Transformation

The software used matrix transformations to align each segment for slicing while retaining its orientation information. After the standard slicing process, the generated toolpath coordinates were transformed back into the machine coordinate system.

G-code Processing

PrusaSlicer was integrated as the standard slicing engine. A custom post-processing workflow transformed the generated G-code into machine-compatible instructions for the 3DP-5A printer.

Experimental Results

The developed slicer was tested through physical printing using a solid cylinder model with a 30-degree bend. The five-axis printer successfully printed the bent cylinder without support structures. During printing, the machine bed rotated up to 30 degrees, with its starting orientation determined by the developed algorithm.

Bambu Lab A1 Mini Comparison

The same STL design was also printed using a Bambu Lab A1 Mini as a comparison. The conventional printer used support structures for the bent geometry.

Dimensional Comparison

Dimensional deviation was evaluated by comparing the physical prints with the original STL design. The measured deviations were:

  • 3DP-5A: 0.95% dimensional deviation.
  • Bambu Lab A1 Mini: 0.885% dimensional deviation.

The measurements show that the five-axis prototype produced a dimensional deviation of 0.95% under the tested conditions, compared with 0.885% for the Bambu Lab A1 Mini print. The comparison also demonstrated the support-free printing capability of the five-axis approach for the tested bent geometry.

Limitations

The implemented orientation planning method relies on layer center points to determine a dominant orientation for each Z-layer. As a result, it cannot represent multiple branches that require different orientations within the same layer. This limits its applicability to more complex branching geometries.

Physical testing was also limited to the available experimental setup. Additional model testing could not be completed after the printer bed was damaged and my hardware teammate was no longer available.

Research Contribution

This work demonstrates the development and integration of a segment-based slicing workflow for a five-axis 3D printer. My primary contribution was the independent development of the slicer software, including its geometric processing, orientation planning, coordinate transformations, graphical interface, and G-code processing workflow.

The physical experiment demonstrated the ability of the developed system to print the tested 30-degree bent solid cylinder without support structures, providing an initial validation of the segment-based approach.
